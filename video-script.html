What this video has to do

This replaces an earlier draft written against the wrong rubric. DATA72000's actual video component is shorter, weighted differently, and asks for something plainer than "reveal what the report can't": Appendix 5 of the handbook describes the audience as non-cognate — a client, a user, a management board — and the job as communicating clearly what the project was and what it achieved.

CriterionWeightWhat it actually asksHow this script answers it
Communication50% Does it clearly explain what the project was about, in an engaging way, without overemphasising detail? One real anecdote to open, one sentence per agent, then the system doing the explaining by running.
Delivery30% Pace, narration quality, and timed within the stated limit (under 5 minutes). Scripted to ~4:20 at a spoken 145 words/minute, with named silences where the system, not the narrator, should hold attention.
AV quality20% Sound and visuals complement the content and are appropriate to it. Screencast for anything the audience should watch happen; talking head for anything the audience should hear you say.

Format chosen: screencast + talking head

The handbook names three likely formats — screencast, video poster, or slides with a talking head — and recommends screencast specifically when the goal is to demonstrate software. This project's clearest evidence is the system running, so segments 3–5 are live capture; segments 1, 2 and 6 are you, on camera, doing the explaining a demo can't.

Pre-flight

Work top to bottom. The GPU check is not optional: this project's own chapter on the corpus states local CPU inference runs roughly seventeen minutes per negotiation, against a GPU tunnel that cuts that by roughly thirty-fold.

  • Start the GPU tunnel and the whole stack. One command. It opens the SSH tunnel, sends a throwaway call to absorb the cold model load, then starts the API without --reload and the frontend. 
    .\scripts\start_demo.ps1
  • Confirm the banner is green before you record. The Negotiate page shows a green “GPU connected” pill and the live ollama_host. Amber means you are on local CPU and the live demo will not fit inside five minutes.
  • Have docs/woods-milton-keynes.txt ready to drag in on camera. There is no standalone Woods PDF in the repo — that case previously lived only pre-baked in src/cases/real_cases.py, reachable via the Cases page, not fresh upload. This plain-text file (the same source text, written out) fixes that; the uploader accepts .txt. Do one dry run first and read what branch the Court actually takes this time (see the note under segment 3) before deciding what to say on camera.
  • Open the Bromcom log and find the passage. Segment 5 depends on it. Have it already scrolled to the right place, font size raised. 
    batch_results/batch_20260816_165719/run_03.json
  • Have the no-Court ablation output ready. Either run it live or have a finished terminal capture on a second monitor to cut to. 
    python tests/run_batch_evaluation.py --case woods-milton-keynes --no-court --runs 3 --rounds 3
  • Close every unrelated tab, notification and chat app. Set display scaling to 125% so the negotiation transcript and compliance cards are legible at 1080p.
  • Do one silent dry run of segment 3 end to end. The only segment that can fail live. If extraction stalls, you want to find that out now, not on the take.

The script

Left lane is what the viewer sees. Right lane is what you say, written to be read aloud at roughly 145 words a minute — a touch slower than conversational, since a non-cognate audience is the one the handbook names. BEAT marks a deliberate pause: let the system be watched, don't talk over it.

1 · The cold open

~26 sec · talking head

On screen

  • Open on you, full frame. No title card yet.
  • At “award letter and scoring table”, cut briefly to a still of the two documents side by side.
  • Back to you for the last two sentences. Title card lands on the final word.

Narration

At a board demonstration for Fusion21, a staff member handed me an award letter and a scoring table from a live procurement and asked what my system made of them.

Ninety seconds later it reported a manifest error, with its working shown. I checked the table by hand. Every row summed exactly. My system was confidently wrong. This project is about that gap.

Why open here. It is true, it is unrehearsed, and it states the whole thesis — a fluent system can be confidently wrong — in under thirty seconds, without a slide.

2 · Who, and what

~39 sec · talking head, then the app

On screen

  • You, full frame, through most of this.
  • Naming the four agents: a small on-screen label appears next to each as you say it — CA, Bidder, Court, Summary.
  • Cut to the live app's home page on the final sentence.

Narration

I'm Shreyas Lawand. This is an MSc Data Science project with Fusion21, a social enterprise running procurement frameworks for housing providers, councils and NHS bodies.

When a losing bidder challenges a contract award, the dispute is slow and expensive. I built a system where three language-model agents negotiate one: a contracting authority, an aggrieved bidder, and a court modelled on the Technology and Construction Court, plus a fourth agent that summarises the outcome. They run on a local Llama 3.1, orchestrated as an explicit decision graph.

Here's that system, live, on a real judgment.

Keep this to one breath per sentence. The Communication criterion rewards clarity over coverage — four agents, one sentence, then get out of the way.

3 · Live — a real judgment, end to end

Screen capture ~65 sec

On screen

  • Navigate to Negotiate. Let the green GPU pill sit in frame for a beat.
  • Drag docs/woods-milton-keynes.txt into the uploader.
  • Extraction runs. The scenario card appears — hover the description so the 60/40 formula and the 40/6-mark correction are readable.
  • Click Start negotiation; let statements and rounds stream in on their own.
  • Scroll to the Court compliance card and highlight the reasoning text, specifically where it explains why it isn't computing a number.

Narration

This is the system live. I'm uploading a real judgment — Woods Building Services against Milton Keynes Council: an eight million pound asbestos contract with a published sixty-forty price-quality split.

Beat — 5 sec, let extraction finish

Extraction turns the document into a structured scenario, keeping that formula exact rather than summarising it away. Now the negotiation. Both parties state their interests and their fallback position before anyone bargains, and every message arrives live, as the model produces it.

Beat — 10 sec, say nothing, let it stream

Here's the court's finding, and it's the more interesting outcome, not the tidier one. It recognises a real formula is in play, checks whether the source also states the sub-scores that formula needs, finds that it doesn't, and refuses to invent them — reasoning instead from what the judgment actually states about the correction. On a laptop CPU, this negotiation takes roughly seventeen minutes. On a university GPU over a tunnel — under a minute.

Do not cut this segment. One unbroken take is the point — it proves the system runs. What it actually does, verified live before writing this line: the Woods source text states the formula and the court's final correction (40 marks off EAS, 6 marks on to Woods) but not the raw sub-scores, so a correctly-behaving Court takes the qualitative branch here, not the clean exact-arithmetic one — don't narrate "it recomputes the score" if you re-test this and get a different branch; describe whichever branch it actually takes on your take. Fallback: if extraction stalls on the night, cut to a pre-recorded take of the same sequence and say nothing about it; keep a clean spare rendered before you start.

4 · Live — what the client actually asked for

Screen capture ~31 sec

On screen

  • Open the risk screen panel on the Negotiate page.
  • Set three dropdowns on camera, using their real labels: Evaluation record documentation → weak, Score margin to the losing bidder → narrow, Debrief you are about to send → minimal.
  • Flags appear. Expand one so the rationale and mitigation are both readable.

Narration

But the simulator only runs after a dispute exists. What Fusion21 actually asked for was to help stop disputes starting.

So this is the pre-award risk screen: sixteen rules, no model call at all, each traced to a sentence in Fusion21's own internal guidance. I'm setting three fields an authority genuinely knows before it sends its standstill letter. Each flag names what triggered it, how confident the system can be, and a concrete mitigation.

The line to land. “No model call at all.” It reframes the project from an LLM demo into something a procurement team could run on a Tuesday.
02:41

5 · What the results actually show

Screen capture ~67 sec

On screen

  • Terminal with the no-Court ablation running or its finished output. Let “deadlock” scroll past repeatedly.
  • Cut to run_03.json in your editor, large font.
  • Highlight progressively as you read: “let's say the scores are as follows” → the invented A=80, B=70, C=90 block.

Narration

Two findings matter more than any accuracy figure. First: on the cases with a real, verified disposition, a majority-class guess, a naive single prompt, and the full pipeline all land on five out of six, missing the identical case. Accuracy alone cannot justify this architecture.

What can is this: remove the court agent, and every run deadlocks — eighteen out of eighteen — because nothing else in the system can decide the parties have converged.

Beat — 3 sec, cut to the log

Second, on fabrication. This is a real, committed log from the Bromcom case. Watch what the court agent does: it writes “let's say the scores are as follows — Bidder 1, A equals 80, B equals 70, C equals 90”, and computes correctly on numbers that appear nowhere in the case. A widened screen now catches thirteen confirmed instances of exactly this shape. Correct arithmetic on invented facts is the failure mode this project exists to catch.

Say the negative result out loud. An examiner who has read the baselines chapter and hears you skip it in the video will read that as spin. Naming it plainly, in your own voice, is what the Communication criterion is actually rewarding here.

Narration

The system can conduct a procurement dispute coherently, and it can be stopped from inventing the arithmetic it reasons from. What it can't yet do is reason better than one well-posed prompt.

Finding that out, honestly, turned out to be most of this project.

If something goes wrong

SymptomCauseFix, in order
Banner amber, extraction crawling SSH tunnel dropped — it dies on sleep, network change, or closing its terminal. Click Connect to GPU in the banner. If that fails, re-run start_demo.ps1. Reconnect takes about a second.
“Could not reach the API server” The API is not running, or is on another port. Restart it without --reload; a reload wipes the in-memory session store mid-negotiation.
“Could not extract a scenario” Scanned PDF with no text layer — the guard fires below 200 characters. Use a text-layer PDF. This guard exists because an empty document once produced an entirely fictional dispute.
Negotiation lost on tab switch Should not happen — session state was lifted to a context provider for this reason. If it does anyway, don't switch tabs mid-take. Use separate OBS scenes instead.

Before you upload

Watch it once at full length with a stopwatch. Check three things: it lands under 5:00 with a little room to spare; the talking head is present in segments 1, 2 and 6, not only at the very start; and someone who has never read the report can say back what the project does and what it found. If the last one fails, cut narration from segment 2 and give the time back to segment 3.
